About

Regional expertise with a wider view of international change

My work examines how states navigate shifting balances of power, economic pressure, institutional change, and the growing political and economic importance of Asia.

I am a PhD student at the Department of International Regional Studies, Higher School of Economics in Moscow, and a Research Assistant at the Laboratory for Contemporary Iranian Studies.

My research combines contemporary history, foreign-policy analysis, political economy, and regional studies. Geographically, my work spans Iran, Southeast Asia, the wider Indo-Pacific, and Eurasia. Thematically, I am interested in how sanctions, economic strategy, regional institutions, connectivity projects, and great-power competition reshape the choices available to states.

I hold a Master’s degree in Asian Studies and a Bachelor’s degree in International Relations from the Higher School of Economics, as well as a Bachelor of Science in International Relations from the University of London.

I work across English, Persian, and Russian.

Research

From regional archives to questions of global order

A major strand of my current research concerns Iran’s engagement with Asia and the historical development of its eastern strategy. I use this work to address broader questions about diversification, sanctions, connectivity, regional institutions, and the adaptation of middle and regional powers to systemic change.

Doctoral Project

The Evolution of Iran–ASEAN Relations, 1997–2024

The project examines how a politically constrained regional power sought diplomatic and economic diversification in Southeast Asia; how ASEAN states and institutions managed relations with Iran; and how sanctions, nuclear politics, regional competition, connectivity projects, and changing global alignments shaped the possibilities for cooperation.

The research draws on archival materials, diplomatic records, policy documents, regional press, and multilingual sources.
